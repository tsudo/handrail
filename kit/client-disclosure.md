# Client disclosure language (Level 1, when AI output reaches clients)

*Use when anything an AI tool produces reaches your clients or customers, even after someone on your team reviews it, or when AI records or transcribes meetings with them. Pick the version that fits, edit it to match what you actually do, and put it where clients will see it: an engagement letter, a statement of work, your terms, or a note at the start of a recorded meeting. Check client contracts first: some restrict AI use on their material, and those terms override this wording.*

## For an engagement letter or statement of work

*Don't sign this until every tool used on client work has training switched off in its terms or settings, including training on "de-identified" data. If one does not, remove that sentence or stop using that tool on client work.*

> We use AI tools to help with research, drafting and analysis. We use only tools on business terms that do not allow the provider to train on your information, and a member of our team reviews everything we deliver to you. We do not put [information you never put in AI, e.g. your customer lists] into AI tools without your written agreement. If you would prefer we did not use AI tools on your work, tell us and we will agree how to proceed.

## For a recorded or transcribed meeting

> We use [tool name] to take notes during this call. It records and transcribes the meeting, and the notes are kept [where] for [how long]. Is everyone comfortable with that? We can turn it off.

*Ask before the recording starts, and turn it off if anyone objects. Some jurisdictions require consent from everyone on the call.*

## For a website or terms of service

> [Organization name] uses AI tools in some of its work. We choose tools that do not train on our clients' information, we review AI-assisted work before it is delivered, and we do not use AI to make decisions about individuals without a person involved. Questions: [contact].

## Keep it true

Disclosure is a promise. If the way you use AI changes, update the wording before the change, not after. A disclosure that no longer matches what you do is worse than none.
