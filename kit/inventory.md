# AI inventory (Level 1)

One row per AI tool, per purpose. This is where the quick-score ratings and the owner's decision live. The spreadsheet version is [inventory.xlsx](inventory.xlsx), with drop-down lists for every rating and decision; [inventory.csv](inventory.csv) is the same columns as plain text. At Level 2 use the [AI register](ai-register.xlsx), which adds exceptions and findings.

*Include AI features inside software you already pay for (meeting notetakers, writing assistants, "copilots"), and any personal accounts people use for work. Those are the ones most often missed.*

**Column notes**

- **ID:** any short label that stays with the row, such as AI-01.
- **Sensitivity to Function:** the seven quick-score ratings (Low, Medium, High or Severe). Write "(u)" after any rating that rests on an unknown answer.
- **Autonomy, Reversibility, Outside content, Disclosure:** the follow-up ratings from the [worksheet](quick-score-worksheet.md). Leave blank when the follow-up did not apply.
- **Trifecta:** "Applies", "Broken" (and the setting that breaks it in Conditions), or "No" (and say which leg is absent, such as "No: no way out").
- **Tier:** the worst of every rating in the row, or Severe if the trifecta applies. The [glossary](../docs/glossary.md) explains the lethal trifecta.
- **Provisional?:** "Yes" if any rating rests on an unknown answer.
- **Tier as first set up:** fill this only when a condition lowered the tier, so the row shows both.
- **Owner's decision:** Allow, Allow with conditions, Banned, or Exception. High tiers are "ask first": the owner decides in writing before anyone uses the tool.
- **Next review:** a year for Low and Medium; six months for High; three months for a Severe exception. The policy uses the same dates.
