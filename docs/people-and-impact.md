# Trust is the risk multiplier

The People surface asks a question most AI assessments skip: when the tool is wrong, who pays for it?

## Reliance matters more than reach

An advisory tool that ten thousand people glance at and verify can be lower risk than an "authoritative" tool three people act on without checking. Audience breadth matters, but trust before verification matters more. Three Surfaces rates reliance from "a person checks every output" to "the output makes the decision."

A review step only counts if it is real. If reviewers approve nearly everything unchanged, the tool is spot-checked at best, whatever the process diagram says. At Level 2, rating reliance Low needs evidence such as edit or rejection rates.

## Count harm outside your own team

Decision impact rates the worst plausible effect on someone outside your team: a person's money, job, health or legal position, or a client's decisions, contracts or confidential information. Harm to your own organization still matters; it goes in the impact assessment, not the score.

For a consulting practice, the real risk of an AI tool is rarely harm to an individual. It is a client's strategy reaching a vendor, or a confident wrong number in a client deliverable. The decision-impact question counts that.

## Disclosure and consent

If a tool records a client meeting, the people on the call should know before it starts. If AI drafts work a client pays for, your engagement terms should say so. A disclosure you have not made, or one that no longer matches what you do, is a People risk in its own right.

## The impact assessment

When any People question is High or above, or the lethal trifecta applies, a Level 2 assessment adds a short impact assessment. It opens with one sentence:

> If [the tool] is wrong, manipulated, or over-authorized, then [specific harm] could occur to [the people or clients affected], resulting in [impact on them and on the organization].

Then it asks who is affected, how they rely on the output, how reversible the harm is, how they will know AI was involved, and what reduces the harm. This is the part that lines up with ISO/IEC 42001 clause 6.1.4 and with ISO/IEC 42005, the standard for AI system impact assessments.
