# Place AI by what it may do

Vendors name their products for marketing. "Assistant," "copilot" and "agent" tell you little about risk. A copilot that can send email on your behalf is not assisting; it is acting. Place every AI tool by the most it is allowed to do.

## The function ladder

| Rung | What it means | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assist | Drafts, rewrites or answers for a person who decides what to use | Low |
| Analyze | Summarizes, classifies or extracts from material a person provides | Low |
| Recommend | Proposes options or a course of action for a person to choose | Medium |
| Influence | Shapes what others see or decide, such as ranking, routing or prioritizing, without acting itself | Medium |
| Take action | Changes systems or sends things, with a person approving or able to stop each action | High |
| Operate autonomously | Acts on its own within set limits, without a person approving each action | High |
| Operate without limits | Acts on its own with no defined limit on what it may do, or creates its own sub-agents | Severe |

Take the highest rung the tool is **allowed** to reach, not the one you usually use. If a meeting notetaker can email summaries to attendees automatically, it takes action, even if you mostly read the summaries yourself.

## Autonomy and reversibility decide the rest

Two tools on the "operate autonomously" rung can carry very different risk. One drafts support replies and sends only the simple ones within a short allowlist of topics; another can issue refunds. Two more questions separate them:

- **Autonomy.** Does a person approve each action, only the riskiest ones, or none? An agent that can create its own sub-agents is Severe.
- **Reversibility.** Can a wrong action be undone? Editing a document can. Sending an email, posting publicly or moving money cannot.

## Agents and connected tools move up and to the right

Connectors, plug-ins and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers give AI tools new abilities quickly, often without anyone reassessing them. Every new connector is a reason to look at the function rung again: what can the tool now do that it could not do last month?
